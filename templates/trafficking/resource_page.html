🚨 Human Trafficking in Kenya

Know the signs. Report safely. Help victims get justice.

Under the Counter-Trafficking in Persons Act 2010 (Cap 61) — maximum penalty: life imprisonment

🏭

Labour Trafficking

Forced to work without pay, freedom restricted. Common routes: domestic work in Gulf states, farms, factories.

Signs: passport taken, wages withheld, not allowed to leave or call family.

🔒

Sexual Exploitation

Recruited under false promises then forced into commercial sex work. Targets young women and girls, often through romance or fake jobs.

Signs: controlled by another person, phone taken, moved between locations.

👶

Child Trafficking

Any child recruited, transported or exploited for labour or sex. Consent of the child is irrelevant under Kenyan law.

Signs: child not in school, working long hours, fearful of adults.

⚠️ Warning Signs

📵 Person's phone or documents are controlled by someone else
💸 Recruited with a promise of high-paying work in Dubai, Saudi Arabia, Qatar
🏠 Many people living together in poor conditions, controlled by one person
😨 Person appears frightened, does not speak freely, looks to others for permission
📘 Passport or ID held by employer or agent — especially on arrival at airport
💬 Recruited through Facebook, WhatsApp, TikTok for domestic work or modelling

⚖️ What the Law Says

Counter-Trafficking in Persons Act 2010 (Cap 61) makes it a criminal offence to recruit, transport, transfer, harbour or receive a person through force, fraud, deception or abuse of power for the purpose of exploitation.

Penalties:
• General trafficking: up to 30 years imprisonment + fine
• Where the victim is a child: up to life imprisonment
• Where the victim dies: life imprisonment
• Organisations found guilty: KES 5 million fine

Victim protection: Victims of trafficking cannot be prosecuted for crimes they were forced to commit during trafficking. The CTiP Secretariat provides repatriation, rehabilitation and legal aid.
